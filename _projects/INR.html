BandRC: Band Shifted Raised Cosine Activated Implicit Neural Representations

A raised-cosine-based activation with learnable bandwidth and band-shift, guided by prior-knowledge embeddings, for high-quality implicit neural representations.

Pandula Thennakoon · Avishka Ranasinghe · Mario De Silva · Buwaneka Epakanda · Roshan Godaliyadda · Parakrama Ekanayake · Vijitha Herath

Paper (arXiv) Code Cite

1. Overview

BandRC introduces a Band Shifted Raised Cosine activation for implicit neural representations (INRs), designed to mitigate spectral bias, improve robustness to noise, and better capture both local and global structures in signals. The activation is the impulse response of a frequency-shifted raised cosine filter with learnable bandwidth T and shift ζ, combined with a prior-knowledge embedding that adapts these parameters to the signal at hand.

Built on a 5-layer MLP with 256 neurons per layer and four BandRC-activated layers, the method is evaluated on image reconstruction, denoising, single-image super-resolution, inpainting, and 3D occupancy fields, consistently outperforming contemporary INR activations such as ReLU+PE, SIREN, WIRE, and INCODE.

2. Method

2.1 BandRC Activation

The activation is derived as the impulse response of a shifted raised cosine filter. For input x, the BandRC activation is 

φ(x) = (1/T) · sinc(x / T) ·
       cos(π β x / T) / (1 − (2 β x / T)²) · exp(2π j ζ x)
  • β (roll-off rate) is fixed (e.g., β = 0.05) for stability.
  • T (bandwidth) and ζ (frequency shift) are learnable and adapt per task.
  • Intermediate representations are complex-valued; the real part is taken at the output layer.

2.2 Prior-Knowledge Embedding

BandRC follows the INCODE-style conditioning strategy: a task-specific encoder extracts prior knowledge from the input signal, which is then used to control the activation parameters.

  • Image tasks: truncated ResNet-34 (ImageNet-pretrained) up to the 5th layer, followed by global average pooling and a small MLP to produce a latent code.
  • 3D occupancy fields: truncated ResNet3D-18 to encode volumetric structure into a latent code.
  • The latent code is passed through a harmonizer network (linear layers + SiLU) to generate raw parameters ĥT, ĥζ per activation.

Parameters are constrained to stable ranges via a sigmoid-based projection: 

θ = a + (b − a) · sigmoid(θ̂),  where θ ∈ {T, ζ}

This removes manual tuning, keeps BandRC parameters within meaningful bounds, and makes the INR signal-adaptive.

2.3 Spectral Bias & Chebyshev Analysis

Using harmonic distortion and Chebyshev polynomial analyses, BandRC is shown to maintain stronger, more stable coefficients over a wider range of polynomial degrees than sinusoidal or Gaussian nonlinearities, implying better high-frequency support.

  • Chebyshev expansions on [-5, 5] show Raised Cosine coefficients stay significant up to ~20 orders, while Sine and Gaussian decay much faster.
  • Chirp-image experiments confirm lower spectral error across frequencies and stronger high-frequency energy in deeper layers compared to SIREN.

3. Experiments & Results

3.1 Image Reconstruction (Kodak)

  • Dataset: Kodak 24 lossless images, native resolution.
  • Average PSNR: BandRC 41.24 dB vs. INCODE 35.57 dB, ReLU+PE 32.79 dB, SIREN 33.09 dB, WIRE 29.41 dB.
  • On Kodak image 20, BandRC improves over INCODE by +8.93 dB and over WIRE / SIREN by more than +12 dB, while converging faster and more stably.

Figure: Qualitative comparison on Kodak image 20 – BandRC vs. existing INR activations.

3.2 Image Denoising (DIV2K)

  • Noise model: photon noise (Poisson, mean count 30) + readout noise (std = 2).
  • BandRC achieves a +0.46 dB PSNR gain over INCODE, with better color preservation and structural integrity in reconstructions.

Figure: Denoising on DIV2K – BandRC vs. baselines under photon + readout noise.

3.3 Single-Image Super-Resolution

On DIV2K, BandRC is evaluated at 2×, 4×, and 6× super-resolution:

ReLU+PE32.8028.8926.29

Qualitatively, BandRC produces sharper details (e.g., butterfly wings) and cleaner backgrounds, especially in the challenging 6× setting.

Figure: Super-resolution results for 2×, 4×, and 6× – BandRC retains high-frequency details better than baselines.

3.4 Inpainting & 3D Occupancy Fields

  • Inpainting: Celtic spiral knots image (572×582), with only 20% pixels observed. BandRC reconstructs continuous knot structures with higher PSNR and competitive SSIM, capturing sparse patterns more reliably than SIREN, WIRE, and INCODE.
  • Occupancy fields: Thai statue on a 512³ grid. BandRC attains IOU = 0.992, slightly surpassing INCODE (0.991) and clearly outperforming ReLU+PE, SIREN, and WIRE.

Figure: Inpainting of Celtic spiral knots with 20% pixels observed – BandRC recovers continuous structures.

Figure: 3D occupancy reconstruction of the Thai statue – BandRC produces clean, high-IOU surfaces.

4. Architecture & Training

  • Default INR: 5-layer MLP, 256 neurons per hidden layer, BandRC activations on 4 hidden layers, optional sigmoid on the output depending on the task.
  • Optimization: Adam; learning rate tuned per task (e.g., 0.01 for 2D reconstruction, smaller for deeper / larger models).
  • Hardware: experiments run on NVIDIA Quadro GV100 (32 GB) using PyTorch.

A width–depth ablation (Kodak image 22) shows PSNR rising with capacity, up to ~52 dB for width = 512, depth = 4 (1000 epochs). A practical configuration with width = 256, depth = 3 still reaches ~39.6 dB while being more efficient.
