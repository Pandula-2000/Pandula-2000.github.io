1. Overview

Implicit neural representations (INRs) encode signals as continuous functions parameterized by MLPs. While they excel at compactly modeling complex signals, their performance is tightly coupled to the choice of activation function and is often limited by spectral bias, poor high-frequency sensitivity, and difficulties in capturing both local and global structure. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

COSMO-INR addresses these issues by:

  • Using harmonic distortion analysis and Chebyshev polynomials to expose how common odd/even activations attenuate parts of the post-activation spectrum.
  • Introducing complex sinusoidal modulation (φ(x)·ejζx) to restore full spectral support across layers.
  • Designing COSMO-RC, a raised-cosine based INR activation with learnable T, ζ, and a ResNet-based prior embedder for parameter regularization.
Key Takeaway. By explicitly controlling the spectrum induced by the activation, COSMO-INR removes structural spectral loss and yields consistent gains across representation and inverse problems.

Highlights

  • Spectral analysis of INRs via Chebyshev expansions and harmonic distortion.
  • Proof that symmetry in activations (odd/even) knocks out half of the Chebyshev coefficients.
  • Complex modulation restores missing modes and enhances the blueshift effect.
  • COSMO-RC: raised cosine + complex modulation + prior knowledge embedder.

2. Method: Complex Sinusoidal Modulation & COSMO-RC

2.1 Harmonic Distortion & Chebyshev Analysis

Prior work shows that nonlinear activations can be approximated via polynomials, where each coefficient contributes to spectral expansion (“blueshift”). COSMO-INR revisits this using Chebyshev polynomials, yielding a stable basis on [−1, 1] and revealing:

  • Even activations: Chebyshev coefficients vanish at odd indices (only half the spectrum is used).
  • Odd activations: coefficients vanish at even indices.
  • As a consequence, post-activation spectra are structurally attenuated, limiting the expressive power of typical INR activations.

2.2 Complex Modulation

To mitigate this structural spectral loss, COSMO-INR defines:

g(x) = φ(x) · ejζx,   φ(x) ∈ ℝ

The real and imaginary parts of g(x) have complementary Chebyshev spectra. Together, they avoid the simultaneous vanishing of coefficients that plagues purely even/odd activations, thereby:

  • Preserving both odd and even spectral components.
  • Strengthening the blueshift effect across layers.
  • Extending usable spectral bandwidth deeper in the network.

2.3 COSMO-RC Activation

COSMO-RC instantiates this idea using a raised-cosine base:

φ(x) = 1/T · sinc(x/T) · cos(πβx/T) / (1 − (2βx/T)²) · exp(2πjζx)
  • Raised cosine base chosen for strong Chebyshev coefficient support and high blueshift.
  • β (roll-off) is fixed (e.g., β = 0.05) for stability.
  • T (bandwidth) and ζ (frequency shift) are learnable per layer.
  • Intermediate feature maps are complex-valued; the final layer takes the real part.

2.4 Prior Knowledge Embedder & Regularization

COSMO-RC uses a task-specific embedder to set T, ζ:

  • Image tasks: first 5 layers of ResNet-34 on the input image.
  • 3D occupancy: first 5 layers of ResNet3D-18 on voxel/point data.
  • Embedder output → small MLP → latent vector → mapped to {T, ζ} across layers.

Parameters are bounded via a sigmoid projection:

θ = a + (b − a) · sigmoid(θ̂),    θ ∈ {T, ζ}

This reduces sensitivity to initialization, shrinks the search space, and allows T, ζ to adapt to the underlying signal statistics during training.

3. Experimental Results & Benchmarks

3.1 Image Representation (Kodak)

On the Kodak image dataset, COSMO-RC significantly improves reconstruction quality:

  • Average PSNR: 41.24 dB vs. 35.57 dB for INCODE.
  • Sharper structures, faster and more stable convergence, particularly on fine details (e.g., aircraft wheels).

3.2 Denoising (DIV2K)

Under photon + readout noise:

  • COSMO-RC obtains a +0.46 dB PSNR gain over INCODE.
  • Better color preservation and structural fidelity compared to prior INR activations.

3.3 Super-Resolution

Single-image SR on DIV2K with scale factors 2×, 4×, 6×:

Method 2× PSNR 4× PSNR 6× PSNR
ReLU + P.E. 32.80 28.89 26.29
SIREN 32.26 29.62 27.31
WIRE 29.02 27.16 25.35
INCODE 32.83 29.96 26.63
FINER 32.94 29.75 27.02
COSMO-RC 34.03 30.42 27.66

COSMO-RC delivers sharper edges, cleaner backgrounds, and higher SSIM (up to 0.96) compared to prior work.

3.4 Inpainting & Occupancy Fields

Inpainting. On a Celtic spiral knots image with only 20% pixels observed:

  • COSMO-RC better preserves structural continuity and recurring patterns.
  • Qualitative improvements over WIRE, SIREN, and INCODE, especially on fine curves and knot junctions.

Occupancy Volume. On the Lucy 3D occupancy task:

  • COSMO-RC achieves higher IOU than prior INR baselines.
  • Captures volumetric details with cleaner boundaries and fewer artefacts.

4. Applications & Use-Cases

4.1 Signal & Image Representation

COSMO-INR is directly applicable to any task where a continuous representation of a discrete signal is required:

  • High-fidelity image approximation (e.g., Kodak, DIV2K).
  • Band-limited signal modeling where spectral control is crucial.
  • Applications where derivatives or smoothness of the representation are important.

4.2 Inverse Problems

Thanks to stronger spectral support and improved optimization behavior, COSMO-RC can be used as a deep image prior in:

  • Image denoising under physically motivated noise (Poisson + readout).
  • Single-image super-resolution at arbitrary continuous scales.
  • Image inpainting from sparse measurements or masks.

4.3 3D & Neural Fields

In 3D, COSMO-INR naturally extends to:

  • Occupancy fields for shape reconstruction (e.g., Lucy model).
  • Neural radiance fields (NeRF) for view synthesis with improved high-frequency detail retention.

4.4 When to Use COSMO-RC

  • You need better high-frequency fidelity than ReLU/SIREN/WIRE/INCODE/FINER.
  • You care about theoretical guarantees around spectral coverage and attenuation.
  • You can afford extra activation cost in exchange for PSNR / reconstruction quality gains.
Efficiency vs. Accuracy. COSMO-RC is more computationally expensive than some baselines, yet consistently achieves higher PSNR (up to +8.9 dB vs. INCODE in some settings), highlighting a deliberate trade-off between activation complexity and representation quality.

5. Architecture & Training Summary

Network Configuration

  • Default INR: 5 layers, 256 neurons each, COSMO-RC activations on 4 hidden layers.
  • NeRF: 8 COSMO-RC layers (256 neurons, real-valued final layer split into density & RGB heads).
  • Loss: standard mean squared error on signal / pixel / occupancy values.

Training uses Adam with typical learning rates around 10−2 for 2D tasks and 5×10−4–8×10−5 for NeRF-style tasks.

Hardware

Experiments were primarily run on NVIDIA GPUs (e.g., Quadro GV100 32 GB), with PyTorch implementations.

Ablations & Efficiency

Layer-width/depth ablation demonstrates increasing PSNR with model capacity (e.g., up to ≈52 dB for 512-wide, 4-layer models). A 256-wide, 3-layer architecture provides a good trade-off between quality and cost.

Efficiency comparisons show:

  • COSMO-RC has higher per-iteration time and lower GFLOPs/s throughput than INCODE and SIREN.
  • Nevertheless, COSMO-RC achieves substantially higher PSNR, confirming the value of richer spectral modeling.
